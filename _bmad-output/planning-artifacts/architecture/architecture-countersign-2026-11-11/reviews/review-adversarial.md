# Adversarial review of the Countersign architecture spine

## Verdict

**No.** Two independent teams building from this spine would not end up with the same behaviour. The spine pins the engine, the policy text, the log's ordering and the input grammar well. It leaves a lot open between units, though: the shared data (event payloads, snapshot, wire protocol, decision records), who owns the derived facts that both the store and the replica compute (class, chain facts, effective scope), what "stale" means, how identities map from Keycloak, Kubernetes and OpenBao, and what OpenBao enforcement means for a grant that has been narrowed or chained. Five of the gaps below can produce a **wrong allow**, the one outcome the PRD calls unacceptable, without any team breaking an AD.

Severity scale: **critical** = two AD-compliant units can produce a wrong allow, or the ADs contradict each other so that one of them must be broken. **High** = different observable decisions, explanations or refusals for the same inputs, or units that can't interoperate. **Medium** = divergence at the edges, or an operational one. **Low** = cosmetic, or a millisecond boundary.

## Critical

### A1 — High-risk decision records "before the answer" contradict "no network call in the check path"
- **Severity:** critical (the ADs contradict each other; any build breaks one of them or invents a third path)
- **Units:** `replica/` (the check path in Quornex and Houston) and `store/` (the only writer to PostgreSQL)
- **ADs both obey:** AD-1 ("`check` and `list` run in-process … with no network call"; "only the installation's store writes … audit"), AD-14 ("records for human-only actions … are written **before the answer**. A failed write refuses"), AD-12 (records carry time and offset)
- **Divergent scenario:** `db-migrate` checks `read secret/prod/orders` (human-only). The replica team in Quornex reads AD-14 as binding: it makes a synchronous gRPC call to the store to persist the record, then answers. The check now has a network call, the latency target is gone, and when the store is down **every** human-only check refuses, even on a fresh replica. Houston's team reads AD-1 as binding: it appends the record to a local fsync'd spool and ships it asynchronously. Then "durable before the answer" means "on this pod's disk", and the record is lost if the pod is evicted. Same inputs, two products: one refuses during a store outage, one allows. And one product's audit trail has holes the other's doesn't. A third team might write the record into the event log as an event, which bumps the state version on every check and streams audit traffic to every replica.
- **Proposed AD fix (new AD-14a, "decision-record path"):** state where records go and how, as one of the two options below, and amend AD-1 to say so.
  - (a) **High-risk checks make one synchronous call to the store to record the decision.** This is the only network call permitted in the check path. A failure refuses. The latency target applies to contained checks only. Or:
  - (b) **The replica writes high-risk records to a local durable spool**, with a defined fsync guarantee and a size and age limit beyond which high-risk checks refuse. A store-side ingester appends them to a **separate audit table, not the event log**, and they don't affect positions.
  - Either way, name the record's schema (see A13) and the package that owns it.

### A2 — "Stale" is undefined: heartbeat received ≠ events applied
- **Severity:** critical (a wrong allow, indefinitely, from a replica that obeys AD-3 and AD-4)
- **Units:** `replica/` as built by the Quornex team, and `replica/` as built by the Houston team (or the replica's stream receiver and its apply loop, if they're built separately)
- **ADs both obey:** AD-3 (the heartbeat is the latest position plus the store's time, every 1 s; resume from the last applied position), AD-4 (two horizons; `now` = the later of two clocks)
- **Divergent scenario:** at position 9,001, Erin withdraws `release-prep`'s grant. Houston's apply goroutine is wedged, say on a poison event or a deadlock in the index rebuild, but its stream goroutine keeps receiving heartbeats `{latest: 9001, time: T}`. Houston's team defines "fresh" as "a heartbeat arrived within the horizon", so the replica stays fresh and keeps allowing the withdrawn grant **forever**. Quornex's team defines "fresh" as "applied position ≥ the latest position in the most recent heartbeat, as of within the horizon", so it refuses high-risk checks after the first horizon. AD-4's "Prevents" says a cut-off replica must fail closed. But Houston's replica isn't cut off: it's behind while connected, and the spine doesn't cover that. The same gap opens just after start-up from a snapshot (A19): is a replica with a snapshot but no heartbeat yet fresh?
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten AD-4):** "A replica's **freshness instant** is the store time `T` of the latest heartbeat whose `latest` position the replica has **applied** (applied position ≥ heartbeat position). Staleness = (AD-4 `now`) − `T`. A heartbeat the replica hasn't caught up to doesn't refresh it. A replica that has received no heartbeat since start-up is stale past both horizons. Stream receipt and apply are measured separately, and only apply counts." Add a layer test: heartbeats keep arriving while apply is blocked, and high-risk checks refuse after the first horizon.

### A3 — No owner of the projection; the `narrowed` payload lets one reader widen a grant
- **Severity:** critical (a wrong allow, from a replica that faithfully implements AD-11's wording)
- **Units:** `store/` (folds the log into state for write-time rules: hand-down "parent still covers", `CreatorHolds`, mayChange, overlap) and `replica/` (folds the same log for checks)
- **ADs both obey:** AD-11 ("a grant's effective scope and end are the grant combined with its **latest** narrowing"), AD-3 (the snapshot is never a second source of truth), AD-5 (the replica recomputes chain facts; the store decides write rules), grant-schema events ("`narrowed`: the grant ID with the new resource **or** new `ends_at`")
- **Divergent scenario:** Grant G covers `api/**` and ends at 12:00. At position 100, Erin narrows the scope to `api/billing` (payload `{resource: api/billing}`). At position 101, she cuts the end to 11:00 (payload `{ends_at: 11:00}`). The store team (copying the spike) merges, so the effective grant is `api/billing` until 11:00. The replica team reads "latest narrowing" literally: the latest is position 101, which carries no resource, so it falls back to the grant's own `api/**`, and the effective grant becomes `api/**` until 11:00. G's holder checks `api/payments`. The store would refuse a hand-down of it. The replica **allows** the check. Both read AD-11 correctly. The chain facts, effective scope, effective end, the closing signal's "created before" comparison, and the class (A9) all get two implementations in two packages, because the layer table gives "chain facts" to the replica while the store needs them too (`HandDown` calls the check path in the spike).
- **Proposed AD fix (new AD, "one projection"):** "A single package, `projection/` (or `state/`), folds events into state and computes every derived fact: effective scope and end, withdrawn, task closed, agent ended, class, floor, admin, authority scopes. Both `store/` and `replica/` import it. Neither re-implements a fold. The snapshot is this package's serialised state at a position, produced and consumed by the same code." Also tighten the event schema: "`narrowed` carries the **full** effective `{resource, ends_at}` after the narrowing, computed by the store, never a partial delta." Add a property test: folding (snapshot at N, then events N+1…M) equals folding events 1…M.

### A4 — Which Keycloak or Kubernetes attribute becomes `<id>` is unspecified; self-approval bypass
- **Severity:** critical (lets a person approve their own request across products; the PRD requires refusal "whichever identity source the approver uses")
- **Units:** the store client or adapter in Quornex, and the one in Houston (each maps its authenticated session to `person:<id>` / `agent:<id>`), plus `store/`
- **ADs both obey:** AD-10 (`PersonID` only from `person:`), AD-18 (products authenticate; Countersign doesn't establish identity), the Identities convention ("`person:<id>` (Keycloak) … the id is `[a-z0-9._-]+`")
- **Divergent scenario:** Quornex maps people to `person:<preferred_username>` (`person:sam`). Houston maps them to `person:<sub>` (`person:3f2a…-…`, a UUID, which also matches `[a-z0-9._-]+`). Sam starts a request through Houston as `person:3f2a…`, then approves it in Quornex as `person:sam`. `approve.forbid-self` compares two different strings and **allows**. Both identities parse, and both products authenticated Sam correctly. Agents are similar: Quornex uses `agent:<serviceaccount-name>`, Houston uses `agent:<namespace>.<name>`, and OpenBao's alias defaults to the service-account **UID** (research-openbao.md). So `agent_ended` from one product doesn't end the other product's grants for the same pod, and two namespaces' `api-refactor` agents share every grant if both use the bare name. Usernames can also be renamed in Keycloak, and then a person's authority silently detaches.
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten the Identities convention into an AD):** "`person:<id>`: `<id>` is the Keycloak `sub` claim of the installation's realm, lowercased. Never the username or email. `agent:<id>`: `<id>` is `<namespace>.<serviceaccount-name>` (or the service-account UID; pick one), and OpenBao's Kubernetes auth role uses the matching `alias_name_source`. One function in `model/` (`FromKeycloakClaims`, `FromServiceAccount`) does the mapping, and products must call it. A conformance test feeds both products' adapters the same token and requires the same `Identity`."

### A5 — OpenBao policy lifetime ignores narrowing, ancestors, signals and staleness
- **Severity:** critical (OpenBao keeps serving a secret that every check refuses)
- **Units:** the `store/` OpenBao reconciler, and `replica/`
- **ADs both obey:** AD-13 ("one policy per grant … covers only the exact path … its `expiration` is **the grant's end**"; "log first, then OpenBao"), AD-11 (the grant record never changes; effective = grant + latest narrowing), AD-5 (the replica recomputes ancestor overlap and closing signals **on every check**)
- **Divergent scenario:** (1) Erin cuts a grant from 2 h to 1 h (decision-table row 11). The replica refuses after 1 h. The reconciler sets `expiration` to "the grant's end", and grant records are immutable, so the end is still 2 h: OpenBao serves the secret for another hour. (2) The parent of a child grant is withdrawn (row 15), or its task closes (row 14). AD-5 makes the replica walk the chain on every check, but AD-13 says nothing about chains: the reconciler team removes the policy only when `withdrawn` names *this* grant, so the child's OpenBao policy survives until its own end. (3) The reconciler's lag isn't bounded, but the replica's is (10 s), so the withdrawal target isn't met for OpenBao. (4) OpenBao evaluates `expiration` on OpenBao's own clock, without AD-4's margin or the later-of-two-clocks rule, so a skewed OpenBao clock lengthens access.
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten AD-13):** "The reconciler's desired state for each grant is a function of the **same projection** the replica uses (A3): a policy exists only while the grant and every ancestor are not withdrawn, task-closed or agent-ended. Its `expiration` is the **effective** end (after narrowing, and the minimum over the chain) **minus** the clock safety margin. Its path is the **effective** scope. Any event that changes these (narrowed, withdrawn, signal_accepted, an ancestor's change) triggers reconciliation of every descendant. The reconciler's lag, from event commit to OpenBao write, has a target equal to the withdrawal target, is measured, and the reconciler emits `policy_written` / `policy_removed` with the event position it reflects." Add an agreement test: for every grant, (OpenBao allows) ⇒ (a replica check at the same instant allows).

## High

### A6 — No owned wire contract: event payloads, snapshot, resume, unknown event types
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** `store/` (producer) and `replica/` (consumer); also the two product store clients
- **ADs both obey:** AD-2, AD-3, the Events convention (names only)
- **Divergent scenario:** the spine names events but not their fields, encoding, version or owner, and the package list has no `api/` or `proto/` (the "store client" in the diagram has no package). The store team ships protobuf `Event{position, type, at, by, oneof payload}`. The replica team expects a JSON payload keyed by `subject`, like the spike's `Event{Subject string}`. Also: the event list has `classification_proposed`, but no confirmation or applied event (the spike emits `resource_classified`, which isn't in the list), no `request_*`, no `task_opened`. Resume "from its last applied position" can mean inclusive or exclusive: one replica applies position N twice, which with append-only markings duplicates a marking, so a later `marking_removed` leaves a copy behind. And when an older Houston library meets an event type added in a newer store, one team skips it (if it's a new restricting event, that's a silent wrong allow), while another halts and goes stale.
- **Proposed AD fix (new AD, "wire contract"):** "A `proto/` package owned by the store team defines the gRPC service, every event's payload as a protobuf `oneof`, the snapshot message (A3) and the heartbeat. The event list in grant-schema.md is closed and normative, and adds `classification_confirmed`, `request_submitted`, `request_approved`, `request_expired` and `task_opened`. Resume is **exclusive**: the store sends positions > N. Apply is idempotent by position: a replica ignores any position ≤ its applied one and halts on a gap. **A replica that receives an unknown event type stops applying and so becomes stale under AD-4 (fail closed); it never skips.** The protocol carries a schema version, and the store refuses replicas below the minimum."

### A7 — Library version skew: two `policies.cedar` live at once
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** `cedarengine/` + `replica/` embedded in Quornex at library version vN, and in Houston at vN+1
- **ADs both obey:** AD-6, AD-8 (policies validated and proved in CI), AD-1 (in-process checks)
- **Divergent scenario:** a rule change ships in library vN+1, say a fixed-rule fix or a new `forbid`. Houston upgrades this week and Quornex next month. The same agent, grant and resource at the same instant are allowed in Quornex and refused in Houston. The store (vN+1, or vN) decides write-time rules with yet another version. Nothing in the spine says the policy set is installation-wide, versioned, or checked for agreement. Replay (A13) can't know which policy decided.
- **Proposed AD fix (new AD, "one policy version per installation"):** "Every policy bundle (`policies.cedar` + schema) has a content hash. The store announces the installation's required policy hash in the snapshot and in every heartbeat. A replica whose embedded hash differs refuses all checks (or refuses high-risk ones and logs; pick one) until upgraded. Every decision record carries the policy hash and library version. Policy changes roll out store-first, behind a store-side switch of the required hash."

### A8 — Installation-wide limits (horizons, margin, hard maxima, depth) have no single owner
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** `store/` and `replica/`; and Quornex's replica configuration versus Houston's
- **ADs both obey:** AD-4 (horizon values deferred), AD-5 ("the replica recomputes expiry **and the hard maximum**"), AD-8 (depth 3), the Deferred list
- **Divergent scenario:** the spike has a `Limits` struct passed to each `State`. In production, Quornex configures `TaskMax=24h, horizon1=10s, horizon2=5m, margin=500ms`, while Houston's Helm chart says `TaskMax=12h, horizon1=5s, horizon2=60s, margin=2s`. A 20 h grant the store accepted is allowed in Quornex and refused in Houston. After 6 s cut off, Houston refuses high-risk checks and Quornex still allows. The store's `ContainedMax` may differ from both. None of this breaks an AD, because the values are deferred and their owner is unnamed.
- **Proposed AD fix (new AD, "limits are installation state"):** "All tunables (`TaskMax`, `ContainedMax`, `ServiceMax`, `AuthorityMax`, `SystemMaxDepth`, `PendingMax`, both horizons, the clock margin, the maximum offset) are set at the store, recorded as a `limits_set` event, and reach replicas through the log and the snapshot. Replicas have no local configuration for them. A replica that has no limits refuses everything."

### A9 — The marking-combination rule (action class) isn't in the spine, and both compute it
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** `store/` (class decides "approved by rules" vs "ask", and hand-down limits) and `replica/` (class decides AD-4's high-risk tier); also two replica teams
- **ADs both obey:** AD-16 ("every covering **classification** counts, so the strictest wins", which is said of the floor, not of markings), AD-17 (the layer computes the class), AD-4 (high-risk = not marked contained)
- **Divergent scenario:** markings: `push repo/api/**` is contained, and `push repo/api/billing` is human-only. The store team uses "most specific marking wins" (the common ACL convention), so a request for `repo/api/billing` is human-only: ask. Another team uses "any human-only covering marking wins" (the spike's `classOf`) and gets the same answer. Now flip it: `repo/api/**` human-only, `repo/api/docs` contained. Most-specific gives contained, approved by rules. Strictest gives human-only. So the store approves by rules (most-specific) while the replica treats the check as high-risk (strictest), or the other way round: the replica treats a human-only action as contained and keeps allowing it up to the second horizon. `marking_removed` has no marking ID either, so "remove which one?" (one of two identical markings, all of them, or by position) is open.
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten AD-16):** "Class = human-only if the action is in the floor of four, **or** any covering marking for that action is human-only, **or** no covering marking is contained. Otherwise contained. Specificity never matters. Markings have store-assigned IDs, and `marking_removed` names one ID. The computation lives only in the projection package (A3)."

### A10 — "A refusal refuses the whole list" can be read two ways; mixed classes when stale
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** `replica/` list path built by two teams (or Quornex's feed and Houston's feed)
- **ADs both obey:** AD-15 ("a refusal or a timeout refuses the whole list"), the Errors convention ("'Refuse' is for what a grant can't do"), AD-4
- **Divergent scenario:** a 10,000-item feed where 9,990 items are allowed and 10 aren't. By the spine's own glossary, a not-allowed item is a "refusal", so team A refuses the whole list and the agent sees nothing. Team B treats only errors and timeouts as list refusals and returns 9,990 items. Second case: the replica is past the first horizon, and the list mixes contained and human-only items. Each single check refuses the human-only ones. Is that a per-item "no" (filter) or a "refusal" (whole list refused)? `TestListCheckMatchesSingleChecks` passes for both teams, because it compares per-item booleans. The timeout value is unstated too.
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten AD-15):** "A list returns, for each item, the boolean a single check would return. Items a single check refuses are left out (filtered), not errors. The whole list is refused, empty with a reason, only when the list itself can't be decided: malformed input (any item non-canonical), an engine error, the replica past the **second** horizon, or the list deadline (value X, installation limit per A8) being exceeded. Past the first horizon, high-risk items are filtered like any other refusal."

### A11 — No read-your-writes: an approved grant is refused right after approval
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** the product's store client and `replica/`
- **ADs both obey:** AD-1 (checks are local), AD-3 (asynchronous push stream), decision-table rows 6–7 (approved by rules, then the check allows)
- **Divergent scenario:** `api-refactor` requests a push (contained). The store replies "approved, grant G". Ten milliseconds later the agent checks the push, before the replica has applied G's event, and gets "no grant; make a request". Quornex's client team blocks on the next heartbeat after each write. Houston's doesn't, so its agents see spurious refusals and file duplicate requests (and fill `PendingPerPrincipal`). The same flow behaves differently across products. The reverse matters more: after a **withdrawal** call returns, the caller's own replica can allow the withdrawn grant for up to the stream lag.
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten AD-3):** "Every store write response carries the log position it committed at. The library offers `WaitFor(position, deadline)`. The check API takes an optional minimum position, and a check that names a position the replica hasn't applied waits up to a deadline, then refuses with reason `replica.behind`. The product clients call it after their own writes."

### A12 — Explanations: which grant is named, which reason wins, IDs for layer refusals
- **Severity:** high (FR-23 and FR-24: the answer, and the replay comparison, differ)
- **Units:** `replica/` built by two teams (also `cedarengine/`'s `Result.Policies` order)
- **ADs both obey:** AD-14 ("an explanation names the engine's rule IDs and the grant chain, up to the first approval"), the Rule IDs convention, AD-9 (the prefilter changes no *decision*)
- **Divergent scenario:** an agent holds two grants that both allow. Team A names the first by log position (the spike). Team B names the one ending latest, or the most specific. For a refusal across three candidate grants (one withdrawn, one expired, one task-closed), the spike reports "the most recent grant tried". But the prefilter changes *which* grants are tried, so even one team's text differs with the prefilter on or off, and `TestPrefilterChangesNoDecision` compares only the boolean. In Cedar, withdrawal and expiry are conditions inside `check.grant-allows`, not `forbid`s, so every chain-fact refusal comes back as `default-deny`. The PRD's "Erin withdrew it at 09:14" must be built by the layer, and its ID and text are left to each team. Refusals from the layer (stale, malformed, broken chain, list timeout) have no IDs at all, yet the Tests convention says "tests assert the ID". The PRD's "who could approve" on a refusal is computed by the store at request time and by nobody at check time.
- **Proposed AD fix (new AD, "explanation contract"):** "A `Decision` has a fixed shape: `{allow, rule_ids (sorted), reason_code, grant_chain, root_approver, chain_fact?: {kind, grant_id, by, at}, approvers?}`. On allow, the named grant is the allowing candidate with the lowest log position. On refusal, the reason is chosen by precedence over **all** candidates (prefilter or not): layer refusal > `forbid` rule > chain fact (withdrawn > task closed > agent ended > expired > narrowed out) > `default-deny`. Layer refusal IDs are a closed list in `model/` (`replica.stale-high-risk`, `replica.stale-all`, `replica.behind`, `input.malformed`, `chain.broken`, `list.timeout`, …). Approvers on refusal come from the projection's `approversFor`, sorted, excluding the principal. Extend `TestPrefilterChangesNoDecision` to compare the whole `Decision`."

### A13 — Decision records lack the log position and policy version, so replay can't reproduce
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** `replica/` (writes decision records) and `store/` or the replay tool (rebuilds state "as it was then", FR-24)
- **ADs both obey:** AD-12 ("each decision record carries the time used and the clock offset"), AD-2 ("the state version is the log position"), AD-14
- **Divergent scenario:** a Quornex replica at applied position 9,000 allows a check at 09:21:00.000. The withdrawal is at position 9,001, committed at 09:20:59 store time, but the replica hadn't received it. The record carries the time and the offset, but not the position. The replay team rebuilds state "as of 09:21:00" by time, includes 9,001, and gets **refuse**: the replay disagrees with what happened, and the audit shows an allow nobody can explain. Another replay team rebuilds "by position at that time" and guesses. Without the policy hash (A7) and the limits (A8), even the right position can replay differently.
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten AD-12 and AD-14):** "Every decision record carries: applied log position, `now` as passed to the engine, the wall clock, the store-time estimate, the offset, the staleness, the policy hash, the library version, and the full `Decision` (A12). Replay = fold events 1…position with the projection package (A3), then call the engine recorded by hash with the recorded `now`. A replay that disagrees with the record is an alert."

### A14 — Task and resource namespaces, and the task lifecycle, have no owner
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** Quornex (emits `task_closed`, names resources), Houston (same), and `store/` (validates "a real task")
- **ADs both obey:** AD-18 (products send closing signals), AD-11 (signals end grants created before them, by position), the Resources convention (canonical paths, with no namespace)
- **Divergent scenario:** Quornex's tracker has task `eng-88`, and so does Houston's (different work). Houston closes its `eng-88`, and every Quornex grant for `eng-88` stops working. Resources: Quornex uses `api/billing` for a repo and Houston uses `api/billing` for a service endpoint, so one grant covers both. Lifecycle: the spike has an in-memory `OpenTask`, with no event and no rule about who may open, re-open or close a task. One store team accepts requests for any task ID (since "real" can't be checked), another refuses until a `task_opened` arrives, and the products don't know they must send one.
- **Proposed AD fix (new convention + AD):** "Task IDs are `<product>:<tracker-id>` (`quornex:eng-88`). Resources start with a registered top-level segment per system (`forgejo/…`, `openbao/…`, `houston/…`), and the registry is installation state. Tasks are opened by a `task_opened` event from the owning product only. A request for a task not open at the store refuses (`task.not-open`). Re-opening is not supported in v1: a closed task ID can't be reopened, and the product must mint a new ID."

### A15 — "Single writer" isn't enforced; write-time rules have a TOCTOU window
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** `store/` as deployed by two installations (or the store's gRPC handler and its log appender)
- **ADs both obey:** AD-1 ("only the installation's store writes"), AD-2 (the next position is taken under a lock in the same transaction), AD-5 (write rules decided once, at the store)
- **Divergent scenario:** a Kubernetes rolling update briefly runs two store pods. AD-2's lock keeps positions gapless, so both pods write happily. Each pod's in-memory projection misses the other's writes, so pod A approves request R while pod B also approves R (two grants). Admins Ann and Bob each remove the other, both pass the "at least two" check against stale state, and zero admins remain. Two reconcilers fight over OpenBao. Even with one pod, a team that validates in the handler and appends later (outside the lock) has the same race between concurrent handlers. Another team validates inside the transaction. Both obey the spine.
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten AD-1 and AD-2):** "The store holds a PostgreSQL advisory lock (leader lease) for its whole lifetime. A process without it serves no writes and runs no reconciler. Each write evaluates its write-time rules against the projection at exactly position N−1, inside the same transaction that appends N, under the position lock. A write whose state moved on is retried."

### A16 — Canonical path, action and agent to OpenBao path, capability and entity: no mapping
- **Severity:** high
- **Units:** the `store/` OpenBao reconciler and `replica/` (via `model.Covers`)
- **ADs both obey:** AD-13 ("covers only the exact path and operations"), the Resources convention (`/**` subtrees), AD-10
- **Divergent scenario:** grant `read openbao/secret/prod/orders/**`. `model.Covers` says the subtree covers its root `…/orders` and everything below it. Reconciler team A writes `path "secret/data/prod/orders/*"`, which doesn't match `…/orders` itself, and OpenBao's `*` is a prefix glob that does match `…/orders-archive`, which the replica refuses. Team B writes `+`-based paths, or adds the KV v2 `data/` and `metadata/` split differently. `read` maps to `["read"]` for one team and `["read","list"]` for another. A grant on an exact path becomes, for one team, a policy that also lists the parent. The agent's OpenBao entity is looked up by alias name (A4). So OpenBao and the replica disagree on the same resource in both directions.
- **Proposed AD fix (tighten AD-13):** "`model/` defines one total function `ToOpenBao(action, resource) → ([]path, []capability) | not-expressible`. `x/**` maps to the two paths `x` and `x/*` with KV v2 `data/` inserted by rule. Never a bare prefix glob. Actions map to a fixed capability table. Anything not mappable is 'not expressible' (rule 10: not created). The agent maps to an entity via the alias source fixed in A4. A table test pins every action, and a property test checks that OpenBao's own glob semantics agree with `model.Covers` on generated paths."

## Medium

- **A17 (medium) — store ↔ replica, AD-4/AD-12:** the store's `now` for write-time rules (hand-down "parent still covers now", authority expiry at approval, `issued_at`) isn't defined. Is it the wall clock, before or inside the position lock? And it has no margin, so the store accepts a hand-down from a parent that every replica already treats as expired (its end within the margin). **Fix:** "The store's `now` is its wall clock read inside the append transaction, truncated to ms. That value is the event's `at` and the grant's `issued_at`. Write-time rules that depend on time apply the same safety margin as AD-4."
- **A18 (medium) — two store-client teams, Errors convention:** a store outage, a gRPC deadline or a store refusal reach the agent as "refuse" from one client (the PRD says that means "no approval could allow") and as an error or "retry" from another. **Fix:** a closed request outcome `{approved, ask, refused, unavailable}` with a machine `reason_code`. `unavailable` is never shown as `refused`.
- **A19 (medium) — two replica teams, AD-4:** the "maximum offset" is deferred with no rule for exceeding it (one team refuses high-risk checks, another logs), and there's no rule for the state between loading a snapshot and the first heartbeat. **Fix:** an offset over the maximum makes the replica stale for high-risk checks. A replica with no heartbeat since start-up is stale past both horizons (see A2).
- **A20 (medium) — replica ↔ cedarengine, AD-5/AD-17:** AD-5 says the replica recomputes "the hard maximum" on every check, but `CheckInput`/`Node` have no `IssuedAt` or `Kind`. One team adds a field (which needs AD-17's registry entry), and another trusts the store's write-time check. They diverge as soon as a hard maximum is lowered. **Fix:** decide whether the hard maximum is a check-time fact. If it is, add `Node.IssuedAt` and `Node.Kind`, with the maximum taken from the installation limits (A8).
- **A21 (medium) — store client ↔ store, AD-10:** "No caller-chosen ID" also forbids an idempotency key. On a gRPC timeout, a client that retries creates two requests or two approvals, and one that doesn't loses the write. **Fix:** allow an opaque per-call idempotency token. It's not an ID of any record, and the store deduplicates on it within a window.
- **A22 (medium) — store ↔ products, AD-2/AD-18:** pending requests, their expiry (`PendingMax`), and the approver list aren't events, so they don't survive a store restart, and the products' approval screens have no defined source (replica? store RPC?). **Fix:** `request_submitted`, `request_approved` and `request_expired` events, with pending requests in the projection, readable from the replica.
- **A23 (medium) — store ↔ products, AD-16/AD-5:** "authority over the resource" (markings, sharing limits, service authorisation, narrowing) is defined only in a spike comment ("only administrators in v1"). A store team may grant it to approval-authority holders, and products may show "Mark contained" to the wrong people. **Fix:** state in an AD that in v1 authority over a resource = active administrator.
- **A24 (medium) — store ↔ products, AD-10:** "the store assigns every ID", but not the format or how uniqueness survives a restart. The spike's in-memory counter (`grant-7`) restarts at 1. **Fix:** IDs are `<kind>-<log_position>` or UUIDv7, fixed in the convention table.

## Low

- **A25 (low) — replica ↔ `engine.Guard`, AD-4/AD-7:** is `now` = truncate(max(clocks) + margin) or truncate(max(clocks)) + margin? They differ by under 1 ms when the margin isn't whole milliseconds. **Fix:** margin in whole ms; add, then truncate.
- **A26 (low) — model ↔ products, Identities convention:** "agents and services through Kubernetes", but only `person:` and `agent:` parse, so services are silently `agent:`. That's fine if stated. **Fix:** say services use `agent:`.
- **A27 (low) — store ↔ replica, AD-11:** after `agent_ended`, a re-created pod with the same ID gets new grants (by position) that are valid. That's intended, but unstated. Duplicate `task_closed` / `agent_ended` signals: "before them" should name the latest. **Fix:** state "the latest accepted signal's position" and that ID reuse after `agent_ended` is allowed.
