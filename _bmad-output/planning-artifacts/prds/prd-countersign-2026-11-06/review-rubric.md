# PRD Quality Review — Countersign

## Overall verdict
The thesis holds up and drives the whole PRD: "A fast refusal is acceptable; a wrong allow is not", access only shrinks and ends without anyone acting. Trade-offs are named honestly, the testable consequences are dense, and open items are tagged rather than hidden. The main risk is the PRD's own output contract. When a check returns "ask" and when it returns "refuse" is internally inconsistent, "high-risk" is never defined, and the speed and scale target is an adjective. All three land directly on the 25-case decision table and the two-engine spike, which the PRD does not yet seed.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Most decisions are stated as decisions, with what was given up: drift is reported, not auto-removed, because "Auto-removal could lock out people who legitimately added access by hand" (FR-28 Notes). List checks are all or nothing (FR-22). A forged closing signal is accepted as "a recoverable denial of service, not a wrong allow" (F5). Contained grants are never shareable in v1, and the child re-requests instead (FR-14). The external dependency in FR-13 is called out in bold as "the rule with the most external dependency" (NFR-5 row 7). The `[OPEN, to check with Danny]` callouts sit at real tensions (F3's reading of "anything requiring a human", FR-12's Sam case), not at safe checkpoints.

Two places smooth over a tension instead of naming it. Recording every check synchronously, fail-closed, is set beside a sub-millisecond p99 with no trade-off discussed. Administrator lifetimes create a possible lockout path that nobody has decided on.

### Findings
- **high** Record-every-check vs. sub-ms latency is unacknowledged (§4.7 FR-29, §5 NFR-3) — FR-29 says "Every check produces a record … A check that can't be recorded is refused", and NFR-3 wants "well under 1 ms at p99" at 10,000 × 50,000. Nothing says whether recording sits on the check's critical path, whether it can be buffered, or what a buffer loss means for "A check that can't be recorded is refused". At this volume it also drives month-long storage (SM-6). This is the biggest performance trade-off in the PRD, and it is invisible. *Fix:* State the decision, or make it an explicit Open Question for the spike: synchronous vs. buffered recording, what "can't be recorded" means (write failed vs. buffer full), and whether the 1 ms budget includes recording.
- **medium** Administrator lapse can lock out an installation (§4.2 FR-9) — "An administrator's authority also has a hard maximum lifetime, but it never lapses silently. Renewal needs another administrator." If every administrator's authority lapses (alert ignored, all on leave), no one can renew, and nothing is said about recovery. "At least two administrators" guards against removal, not against expiry. *Fix:* Add a consequence that says what happens when the last valid administrator lapses (refuse with a break-glass path for the architecture, or a staggered-expiry rule), or log it as an Open Question.
- **medium** No stated v1 behaviour if Q6b comes back "no" (§4.3 FR-13, §7.1, §9 Q6b) — The gate dependency is honestly flagged. But the PRD doesn't say what v1 does if Quornex does not route every production merge or sync through a check: block launch, ship with a stated gap like Argo CD in §7.2, or rely on the backstop alone. *Fix:* Add one line to FR-13 Notes naming the fallback posture.

## Substance over theater — strong

The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD. It names Quornex, Houston, Keycloak, Kubernetes and OpenBao, the four ways a grant ends, and the asymmetry thesis. JTBD is five one-liners with no persona padding, and the one undeveloped role ("Customer admins: `[OPEN]` their role") is marked open instead of being filled in. The NFRs are product-specific (two-step staleness, provability per rule, clock skew can "only shorten access"). There is no differentiation or innovation section, and none is needed. The NFR-5 construction-vs-tests table is real design substance.

### Findings
- **low** SM-9 measures configuration, not Countersign (§8 SM-9) — "About 90% of requests are approved by rules alone. This depends on how many actions are marked contained." Countersign doesn't control markings, so the number reflects customers' marking choices. It is labelled secondary, which limits the harm. *Fix:* Either recast it as "rule approval adds no human latency (p99 under X s)", or keep it and say explicitly that it is an adoption signal, not a product target.
- **low** SM-C1 bundles two metrics, and one of them is already primary (§8 SM-C1) — "the number of contained markings, plus wrong allows in the decision table staying at 0." Zero wrong allows is already SM-8. The real counter-metric (marking count) has no threshold or trend direction. *Fix:* Make SM-C1 the marking count (or the share of actions marked contained) with a review trigger, and drop the SM-8 restatement.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is explicit, and every feature serves it: default deny (NFR-1), grants as the only source of allow (FR-2), monotonic shrinking (FR-4, FR-14), cascade (FR-15, FR-19), and explainability (FR-23, FR-24, FR-29). Service grants are the one place access can outlive its creator, and the PRD treats that as a risk to close ("This closes the escape", FR-16 Notes) rather than as a feature. The primary SMs validate the thesis (every grant ends, withdrawal cascades, 0 wrong allows), not activity. Every primary metric has a counter-metric, and SM-C3 (over-caching) and SM-C4 (slowest replica) are sharp ones. The MVP is a problem-solving scope, and the out-of-scope items each come with a reason and a stated gap ("that gap is stated, not hidden", §7.2).

No findings at this level. SM-8 is covered under Downstream usability.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Almost every FR has concrete "Consequences (testable)", several with named test cases (FR-13 attack, FR-19 five-grant cascade, FR-24 before/after replay, FR-28 hand-added access). That is well above typical. But the central output contract is ambiguous, and several thresholds are adjectives or undefined terms. These are exactly what the decision table and the spike have to turn into expected values.

### Findings
- **critical** The boundary between "ask" and "refuse", and between check and request, is inconsistent (§3 Glossary "Check", FR-5, FR-10, FR-13, FR-21) — FR-21 says "'Refuse' is for something the grant can't do, and 'ask' is for something a new approval could make allowed". Yet a check against a withdrawn grant is "refuse" while "A new request for the same access is 'ask'". FR-5 says "A check on an action with no marking returns 'ask'", and FR-13 says an agent "still gets 'ask' when it attempts that action". So a principal that never had a grant gets "ask", but one whose grant was withdrawn gets "refuse" for the same action. FR-10 says "A request for any other action returns 'ask'", which conflicts with FR-1 (malformed or over-long requests are refused) and FR-21 (self-approval is "refuse"). It is also unspecified what a check on a contained action with no grant returns: "ask", an auto-created grant, or "refuse". A 25-case table with expected answers can't be filled in reliably until this is settled. *Fix:* Add a short decision rule (ordered: invalid input → refuse; forbidden by a fixed rule → refuse; matching valid grant → allow; no grant but approvable → ask; grant exists but doesn't cover → ?) and state whether "check" and "request" are separate calls. Then align FR-5, FR-10, FR-13 and FR-21 with it.
- **high** "High-risk checks" is undefined (§5 NFR-2, NFR-6) — Both staleness steps and the clock-offset rule depend on "high-risk checks are refused", and NFR-2 contrasts it only with "contained actions". It isn't in the Glossary. It could mean the floor of four, all human-only actions, or everything not contained. *Fix:* Define it in the Glossary. "Every check on an action that is not marked contained" seems to be the intent.
- **high** The speed target is an adjective, and the scale is ambiguous (§1 Vision, §5 NFR-3, §8 SM-5) — "well under 1 ms at p99" has no bound the spike can pass or fail. "10,000 installations with 50,000 grants each" is unclear for a library where "Each installation has its own permission store" (§7.2). It could mean one replica holding one installation's 50,000 grants, or a single process serving 500M grants. These differ by orders of magnitude in memory. *Fix:* State a number (e.g. p99 ≤ 500 µs, measured on hardware X) and state what one library instance holds during the measurement.
- **medium** "Rules" approving contained actions have no content (§4.3 FR-10, §3 Glossary "Contained") — FR-10 says rules approve, but it never says what a rule checks before approving. Is there a duration cap below the hard maximum? Must the task be valid? Is there any rate limit? With conditions out of scope (§7.2), "rules" may just mean "contained ⇒ approve", but the PRD should say so. The word "rules" is also used for the fixed invariants in NFR-5 ("fixed rules"), and neither sense is in the Glossary. *Fix:* List what the approval rule verifies for a contained request, and add "Approval rule" and "Fixed rule" to the Glossary.
- **medium** Unbounded triggers (§4.5 F5 and FR-20 "A flood of closing signals from one source raises an alert"; §4.2 FR-9 "an alert fires before it lapses") — "Flood" has no threshold or window, and "before" has no lead time. Neither can be tested as written. *Fix:* Give tentative values (e.g. more than N signals per minute per source; alert 14 days before lapse) tagged `[tentative]`, or move them to §9 for the architecture.
- **medium** The clock start for closing signals is ambiguous in an embedded library (§4.5 FR-20, §8 SM-2) — FR-19 is precise ("The clock starts at the withdrawal, not when a replica hears of it"). FR-20 and SM-2 instead use "of Countersign receiving it" / "learning that the task closed". Because Countersign is a library in every product, "Countersign receiving" could mean the permission store or any replica. *Fix:* Say explicitly that the 10 s clock starts when the permission store accepts the signal, mirroring FR-19.

## Scope honesty — strong

The Non-Goals (§6) do real work ("A central permission server", "A translator into Keycloak or Kubernetes RBAC", "An undo system"). Every §7.2 exclusion states its interim consequence (e.g. "agents get no Argo CD access through Countersign, and production syncs stay with people"). Open items are dense, about 15 Open Questions plus inline tags. For a draft whose next step is an architecture spike, that is appropriate, and most items are routed to "For the architecture or the spike". The provenance notes (FR-29 and NFR-6 "drafted with Claude's help … reviewed and adopted by Skander", FR-18 "comes from Skander's own earlier schema work, not from Danny") are unusually honest.

The only gaps are Assumptions Index roundtrip mismatches (see Mechanical notes) and the missing FR-13 fallback (under Decision-readiness).

## Downstream usability — adequate

A glossary exists and declares "No synonyms", the UJs have named protagonists (`api-refactor`, `db-migrate`, Erin, the security team), and most FRs trace to a UJ. But the architecture's main deliverable, the 25-case decision table, has almost nothing to extract from the PRD. Several load-bearing terms are also missing from the glossary.

### Findings
- **high** The 25-case decision table isn't seeded (§8 SM-8, FR-12, FR-13, FR-14) — SM-8 says "All 25 checks, including the 5 attacks", but the PRD names only one attack (FR-13's ten-config-file split) and two "decision-table case" mentions (FR-12 Sam, FR-14 sharing a contained grant). The other four attacks and about 20 cases are unnamed, so the architecture has to invent what the PRD is supposedly validated against. *Fix:* Add a short list of the 25 cases (or at least the 5 attacks), each linked to its FR and expected answer, or say explicitly that the architecture owns the list and SM-8 is validated against that.
- **medium** Key terms are missing from the Glossary, and synonyms drift (§3) — "Authority over a resource" is used in FR-4, FR-6, FR-7, FR-16 and FR-17, and FR-9 says administrators "hold the first authority over the installation's resources". It is distinct from "Approval authority" but never defined. "High-risk", "approval rule" / "fixed rule", "feed" and "item" (FR-22) are also absent. "Hand down", "share" and "delegation" are used interchangeably despite "No synonyms". *Fix:* Add "Authority over a resource", "High-risk", "Approval rule" and "Fixed rule". Pick "hand down" (or "delegate") as the verb, and keep "allowed to share" only as the permission's name.
- **low** FR-22 traces to the wrong journey (§4.6 FR-22) — "Realizes UJ-1", but UJ-1 is a push to a feature branch and has nothing to do with list or feed reads. No UJ covers the list check. *Fix:* Drop the trace, or add a one-line UJ for an agent reading a feed.

## Shape fit — strong

This is an internal library with a capability-spec shape. The UJs are scenarios that carry real test content (UJ-3's cascade, UJ-4's replay), not consumer-experience theater, and there are only four of them. The FRs with testable consequences are the right primary vehicle. Because the PRD is chain-top (it feeds the architecture), the traceability expectations in the previous section apply, and it mostly meets them.

## Mechanical notes
- **FR ordering:** All IDs FR-1 to FR-29 exist and are unique, but they are out of order. FR-18 sits between FR-15 and FR-16 (§4.4), and FR-29 sits between FR-25 and FR-26 (§4.7). Renumber, or add a note that the IDs are stable and not ordinal.
- **FR-16 duplicates:** "A service grant names an owner (a team or role)" appears twice. "Every creation by an agent is audited" and "Every creation is audited" overlap. "The creating agent never approves its own service grant" restates "never by its creator" in the next-but-two bullet. Merge them.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip (§10):** "FR-13 and §7.1 `[ASSUMPTION]`" is indexed, but inline FR-13 is tagged `[OPEN, to check with Danny]` and §7.1 has no tag. NFR-5 is indexed as a proposal but has no inline `[PROPOSAL]` tag. The "Tentative targets" entry lists SM-2/3/4, but the same 10 s `[tentative]` also appears inline in UJ-3, FR-19, FR-20 and FR-26.
- **Open Questions:** Q15 ("Not yet proved: …") is a statement, not a question. The 6b and 9b numbering suggests late inserts, so renumber before publishing. The inline `[OPEN]` tags map cleanly to Q1, Q4, Q6, Q13 and Q14.
- **Scaffolding left in:** "_Draft in progress. Sections are added as discovery proceeds._" (line 10) and "_Skeleton: the FRs are written feature by feature._" (§4). The frontmatter says `updated: 2026-11-06`, but the content cites decisions from 2026-11-11.
- **Formatting:** NFR-6's heading directly follows the NFR-5 summary paragraph without a blank line (§5), which some renderers won't treat as a heading.
- **Addendum:** It correctly notes Danny's email is paraphrased. Every Danny-sourced decision in the PRD (FR-8, FR-9, FR-16, the "Allowed to share" glossary entry) matches the addendum.
